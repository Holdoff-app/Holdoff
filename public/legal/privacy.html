Privacy Policy

Effective 2 August 2026 · Last updated 2 August 2026

This explains what HoldOff does with your messages. It is written to be read, not to be survived. If you only read one section, read “Your draft goes to Google” — that is the part people don't expect.

The short version

  • When you ask HoldOff whether to send a draft, the draft and up to the last 12 messages in that conversation — including the other person's messages — are sent off your phone to be analysed by Google's Gemini AI.
  • Almost everything else stays on your phone: your whole message history, your contacts, your trusted-contacts list, your counters, your quiz result.
  • No name, phone number, email address, contact name, device ID, advertising ID or location is ever sent with a draft.
  • There is no analytics SDK, no crash reporting, no ad network, and no tracking in the app.
  • An account is optional. HoldOff works fully — including the pause — without one.
  • We can't currently tell you exactly how long the analysed text is kept. We explain why in “How long it's kept” instead of guessing.
  • HoldOff is for adults. 18+ only.

Your draft goes to Google. So do the other person's messages.

When you ask HoldOff to look at a message before you send it, this is what happens:

  1. HoldOff takes the draft you wrote.
  2. It takes up to the last 12 messages of that conversation, trimmed to 4,000 characters. That includes the messages the other person sent you — their actual words, not a summary.
  3. If you took the attachment-style quiz, it adds your quiz result.
  4. It sends all of that as text to https://api.smsholdoff.com/api/analyze, a server we run, which passes it to Google's Gemini API and sends the answer back to your phone.

The same thing happens when you talk to the in-app companion: what you type, plus up to the last 12 turns of that chat, plus your quiz result if you have one, goes the same route.

Say the uncomfortable part out loud

The other person in that conversation did not agree to any of this. They texted you. They have no idea their words are being read by an AI model, and they had no way to say no.

We're not going to dress that up. If you use HoldOff on a conversation, you are making a decision about someone else's words as well as your own. Under UK and EU data protection law, you are the one who chose to share their message with us, and we process it as part of giving you the service you asked for.

What we do to keep it as small as it can be:

  • We send text only. Not their name. Not their number. Not the conversation's ID. The messages are labelled only “You:” and “Them:”.
  • We send at most 12 messages, and at most 4,000 characters of them — not the thread, not your history, not your other conversations.
  • We send only when you ask for a verdict. HoldOff does not analyse messages in the background, and does not analyse conversations you never asked about.

What we can't do is promise the other person's words are deleted the moment they're used. See “How long it's kept”. If that's not acceptable to you for a particular conversation, don't run a verdict on that conversation. The pause, the countdown and the trusted-contact prompt all work without it.

Messages can be sensitive by nature

HoldOff is built for people with anxiety, ADHD and people in recovery. A draft you're anxious about at 2am can easily contain information about your health, your relationships, your recovery or your beliefs — the kind of data the law treats as special category data. Your attachment-style quiz result is similar.

Because of that, the lawful basis we rely on for sending message content to be analysed is your explicit consent, given when you choose to run a verdict. You can withdraw it at any time by not running verdicts, and by turning the feature off. Withdrawing does not undo an analysis that already happened.

What never leaves your phone

This is the part we're proud of, so it deserves to be clear. All of the following is read or stored only on your device, in the app's private storage, and is never uploaded anywhere:

  • Your SMS messages. HoldOff reads them through Android permissions to draw your conversation list and threads. They stay in Android's own message database on your phone. Only the excerpt described above, only when you ask for a verdict, ever goes anywhere.
  • Your contacts. HoldOff reads contact names and numbers so a conversation shows a name instead of a number. Your contact list is never transmitted, never uploaded, never backed up to us.
  • Your trusted-contacts list — the names and numbers you type in yourself.
  • Your counters — how many drafts you've checked, how many times you held off, the day you first used the app.
  • Your attachment-style quiz result (a copy is sent with an analysis, as described above; the stored result itself lives on the phone).
  • Your settings, including how long the hold countdown runs, and whether you've finished onboarding.

And there is nothing else running in the background collecting you. The Android app contains no analytics SDK, no crash reporting, no advertising SDK and no attribution SDK — no Firebase, no Crashlytics, no AdMob, no Meta SDK, no Sentry, no Mixpanel, no Amplitude, no AppsFlyer. We don't read your advertising ID. We don't collect your location. There is no tracking pixel in the app.

How long it's kept — including what we don't know

We'd rather tell you the truth than a comfortable number.

On your phone

Everything stored locally — messages, contacts, trusted contacts, counters, quiz result, settings, your session token — stays until you delete it in the app or uninstall HoldOff. Uninstalling removes the app's local storage. We have no copy.

Your account

If you have an account, your email address and password hash are kept for as long as the account exists, and deleted when you delete the account. There are no backups. The database sits on Supabase's Free plan, which does not include the daily backups their paid plans get. That cuts both ways, and you should know both halves: nothing you delete survives in a restore point we hold, and equally, if the database were ever lost we could not bring your account back.

The text sent for analysis — the honest answer

Two things happen to that text, and we can only account for one of them today.

Our analyse server. The text passes through a server we run at api.smsholdoff.com. We have not verified whether that server writes prompts to a log. Its source has not been inspected. We are not going to tell you it logs nothing when we haven't checked — so treat it as possible that a copy of the text you sent exists in a server log until we can say otherwise. Your rights, and how to actually use them

If you're in the UK or EEA you have all of these by law. If you're elsewhere, including in US states with privacy laws, we'll honour them anyway — it's simpler and it's fairer.

  • Access. Ask what we hold about you and get a copy.
  • Correction. Tell us something is wrong and have it fixed.
  • Deletion. Have your account and the data tied to it erased. Start at our data deletion page.
  • Objection. Object to processing we do on a legitimate-interests basis.
  • Restriction. Ask us to hold processing while a dispute is sorted out.
  • Portability. Get the data you gave us in a machine-readable file, or ask us to send it somewhere else.
  • Withdraw consent. Stop running verdicts, and revoke the SMS and contacts permissions in Android settings. Withdrawal applies going forward; it doesn't reverse an analysis already run.
  • No automated decisions with legal effect. The verdict is advice. It never sends, blocks or deletes anything on its own — the send button is always yours.

To use any of these, email hello@smsholdoff.com from the address on your account, or from any address if you don't have an account and tell us what you need. We'll reply within 30 days. It's free. We will not charge you, and we will not make the app worse for you because you asked.

One honest limit: most of what HoldOff knows about you is on your phone and not accessible to us. For local data, deleting it is instant and entirely yours — clear it in the app or uninstall.

The permissions HoldOff asks for, and why

  • Read SMS / Receive SMS / Receive MMS / Receive WAP push — to show your conversations and to notice a new message arriving. This is the app's whole purpose.
  • Send SMS — so you can send the message from inside HoldOff once you've decided to.
  • Read contacts — so conversations show a name rather than a number. Never transmitted.
  • Internet / network state — to reach the analyse endpoint.
  • Notifications — to tell you a message arrived, and to bring back a hold you started.

Making HoldOff your default messaging app

HoldOff asks to become your default SMS app. While it is, it receives your incoming SMS, writes them into Android's own message database on your phone, and sends SMS on your behalf when you tap send. You can hand the default role back to another app at any time in Android settings, and HoldOff will stop receiving your messages.

A limitation you should know before you switch: while HoldOff is your default messaging app, incoming MMS is not retrieved. Picture messages and group messages will not arrive. This is unfinished work, not a design choice, and we'd rather you heard it here than found out by missing something.

Security, described accurately

Everything the app sends goes over HTTPS. Data stored on your phone lives in the app's private storage, which Android keeps separated from other apps, and is protected by your device's own encryption and lock screen. Your session token and settings are stored there in the standard Android way, without an extra layer of encryption on top.

We're a one-person operation. We're not going to claim enterprise security posture we don't have. What we will do is keep the amount of data that ever leaves your phone as small as we can, which is the only security control that can't fail.
