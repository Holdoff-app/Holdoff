Delete your account and your data

HoldOff · Effective 2 August 2026

This page is meant to actually work, not to exist. Below is what to do, what each step really removes, and the parts we can't pull back — said plainly, because you deserve to know where your words ended up.

The two-minute version

  1. Most of your HoldOff data is on your own phone. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage, removes it. We never had it.
  2. If you made an account, email hello@smsholdoff.com from the address on the account. Exact wording is below.
  3. Drafts already checked by the AI can't be recalled. Once text has gone to Google's Gemini API, it's out of our hands. Why, in detail.

Where your data actually lives

HoldOff was built to keep as little as possible off your phone. That's not just a privacy stance — it changes what "delete my data" means, because there isn't much on our side to delete.

What HoldOff never sends anywhere, at all: your name, phone number, email address, contact names, device identifiers, advertising ID, location, or the conversation's thread ID. There is also no analytics, no ad SDK and no crash reporting in the Android app, so there is no behavioural profile of you to delete.

Deleting the data on your phone

Two options. Clearing storage keeps the app but wipes it back to new. Uninstalling removes both.

Option A — clear HoldOff's storage (keep the app)

  1. Open Settings on your phone.
  2. Tap Apps (on some phones: Apps & notifications, then See all apps).
  3. Find and tap HoldOff in the list.
  4. Tap Storage (or Storage & cache).
  5. Tap Clear storage (on some phones: Clear data), then confirm.

Clear storage, not just clear cache. Clearing the cache alone frees up space and deletes nothing that matters here.

This removes your trusted-contacts list, your counters, your quiz result, your onboarding flag and any signed-in session held on the device. The app stays installed and starts over as if new. It does not delete a server-side account, and it does not delete your text messages.

Option B — uninstall HoldOff

If HoldOff is currently your default SMS app, change that back first. Go to Settings → Apps → Default apps → SMS app (the exact path varies by phone) and pick your other messaging app. Otherwise you may end up with no default messaging app and miss incoming texts.

  1. Change your default SMS app back, as above.
  2. Press and hold the HoldOff icon on your home screen or app drawer.
  3. Tap App info (or drag it to Uninstall).
  4. Tap Uninstall, then confirm.

Or from Settings: Settings → Apps → HoldOff → Uninstall.

Uninstalling removes the app and everything in its private storage. Android does not keep a backup of it for us, and we never had a copy.

One thing uninstalling does not do

It does not delete your text messages. Your SMS live in Android's system message database, which belongs to your phone, not to HoldOff. While HoldOff was your default messaging app it wrote incoming messages into that database — and those stay there after HoldOff is gone. Your next default messaging app will show them.

If you want a conversation gone, delete it in whichever messaging app you're using now. And remember your carrier and the other person still have their own records; nothing on this page reaches those.

Deleting your server-side account

You only have an account if you deliberately created one — HoldOff works fully without one. If you never signed up, there is nothing on our servers to delete, and you're done after the steps above.

If you did sign up, we don't have a self-serve delete button in the app yet, so this runs through email. To make sure we're not deleting the wrong person's account, send it from the email address the account is registered to. That address is the only proof of ownership we have.

You don't have to give a reason, and we won't ask for one or try to talk you out of it. If you want to tell us why, we'll read it, but it changes nothing about the deletion.

What we delete

  • Your account record in Supabase — email address and password hash.
  • Your session records.
  • Your premium entitlement flag, if you have one.
  • The deletion correspondence itself, once the request is closed and the retention window has passed.

That is the complete list, because that is all the account is. It does not hold your messages, your drafts, your counters, your trusted contacts or your quiz result — none of that ever syncs to our servers.

What deleting the account does not do

  • It doesn't clear your phone. The app's local data stays until you clear storage or uninstall. Do that too.
  • It doesn't delete your text messages. Those are your phone's, and your carrier's.
  • It doesn't reach back into the AI. Drafts already analysed are covered below.
  • It doesn't undo a purchase — there are none. No payment processor is integrated in the app and premium cannot be bought, so there's nothing to refund or cancel.

How fast we'll respond

We'll acknowledge your request within 7 days, and complete the deletion within 30 days of confirming it's really you. That matches the one-month deadline the GDPR sets. It is the outside limit, not the target — one person reads that mailbox and most requests are done the same week.

If you haven't heard back in that window, send the email again — one person reads that mailbox, and things occasionally get buried. Chasing us is not a nuisance.

The part we can't get back: text already sent to Google

When you asked HoldOff to check a draft, that text left your phone. We cannot reach into Google's systems and pull it out. Nobody can promise you otherwise, and we're not going to.

Here's the whole chain, so you can judge it yourself.

Each check sent: the draft; up to the last 12 messages of that conversation, truncated to 4,000 characters, including messages the other person sent you; and your attachment-style result if you'd taken the quiz. For the companion chat, what you typed plus up to the last 12 turns. It went to our endpoint at api.smsholdoff.com on IONOS hosting, which forwarded it to Google's Gemini API.

What that means for deletion:

  • Google's retention is governed by Google, not by us. How long they hold API content, and whether it can be used to improve their models, is set by Google's API terms and by the API tier the request was made on.   • We have not verified whether our own endpoint keeps a copy. The analyse service on IONOS forwards the text; we have not inspected whether it logs the prompt.   • There is a real limit on what could be found even in principle. HoldOff never sends your name, phone number, email, contact names, device ID, advertising ID, location or thread ID. So a request to check a draft carries no identifier that ties it to you. That is good for your privacy — and it is also exactly why an old request can't be located and deleted by name. The privacy protection and the deletion limit are the same fact.

The one thing entirely in your control is whether any more of it goes: see withdrawing consent.

Withdrawing consent without deleting anything

If you don't want to delete your account or lose your settings, but you do want your text to stop leaving your phone, you can turn things off one at a time.

  • Turn off AI analysis. Settings → AI analysis in the app, and turn off Send my text to Google. That is the switch: one tap, no confirmation step, and it takes effect on the very next request. The page also shows you the date of your current decision. With it off, HoldOff refuses to send your text even if something in the app asks it to.
  • Or just stop asking for checks. Nothing is sent unless you ask HoldOff to look at a draft or you type to the companion chat. If you don't use those, nothing leaves your phone.
  • Stop being the default SMS app. Settings → Apps → Default apps → SMS app, choose another app. HoldOff stops receiving and sending your messages. (This also restores incoming picture and group messages, which HoldOff does not retrieve.)
  • Revoke permissions. Settings → Apps → HoldOff → Permissions, and turn off SMS, Contacts or Notifications. The app will lose features that depend on them, which is the point.
  • Take the app off the network. Settings → Apps → HoldOff → Mobile data & Wi-Fi (path varies by phone) and turn off background and mobile data. The AI check can't run without a connection.
  • Sign out in the app, and your session token is removed from the device while the account itself stays.
  • Clearing just your quiz result or just your counters is not possible today. The app has no control for it — signing out removes your session token, but leaves the quiz result, the counters and your trusted contacts in place. The only route is clear storage, which removes all of the local data at once. If you want one thing gone, that is the cost.

You can also email hello@smsholdoff.com and say you're withdrawing consent for AI processing. Withdrawing consent isn't retroactive — it stops future processing; it doesn't undo what's already been sent.

What we keep after deletion, and why

Deleting means deleting. There's no shadow copy, no "deactivated" account we quietly hold onto, and nothing of yours gets sold or handed on.

Two things people usually ask about, answered plainly:

  • Backups — there aren't any. People assume a deleted account lingers in a nightly backup somewhere. Here it doesn't: the database runs on a plan with no backup retention, so there is no restore point holding a copy of your account once it's gone. The flip side is that we can't recover it for you either. Deletion is final.
  • Records of the request itself. Where the law requires us to show we handled a deletion request properly, we keep a minimal record that the request was made and completed — the date and the fact of it — rather than the content of your account. We keep that record for two years, then delete it too.

Beyond that, we may retain something only if we're legally obliged to — for example a specific legal hold or a valid legal request. If that ever applied to you, we'd tell you unless we were legally barred from doing so.
