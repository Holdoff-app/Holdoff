5. Sideloaded and debug-signed: what that means for you

Read this before you install

HoldOff is distributed today as a sideloaded APK, and that APK is debug-signed. It is not on the Google Play Store.

You are installing software from outside the app store. In plain terms:

  • No Play Store review. Google has not reviewed this build. None of Play's safety, policy or malware checks have been applied to it.
  • No automatic updates. Nothing will update HoldOff for you. If there's a fix — including a security fix — you have to come back and install the new APK yourself.
  • Debug-signed. A debug signature is a development signing key, not a release key. It is not a mark of a verified publisher, and Android may warn you about it. Your device or a security tool may flag the app or refuse to install it.
  • You had to lower a setting to install it. Installing an APK means allowing installs from an unknown source. Turn that permission back off afterwards, and only ever install an APK you actually meant to download.
  • Only install it from us. Get the APK from smsholdoff.com. A copy of "HoldOff" from anywhere else is not something we can vouch for, and a debug-signed app is easier for someone else to tamper with and re-sign.
  • A future Play release may not upgrade this one. If HoldOff is ever published on Google Play, it will be signed with a different key, and Android will most likely refuse to install it over the sideloaded version. You may have to uninstall first — which, as the deletion page explains, wipes the app's local data.

If any of that isn't a trade you want to make, that's a completely reasonable call. Wait for a store release.

7. Making HoldOff your default SMS app

For the pause to work at the moment you hit send, HoldOff asks to become your device's default SMS app. Android only allows one at a time, so saying yes moves your texting from your current app to HoldOff.

Here is exactly what you're agreeing to when you allow that. Please read all of it before you tap yes.

What HoldOff will be able to do

  • Receive your incoming SMS and write them into the Android system SMS database.
  • Send SMS on your behalf — that's how a message actually goes out after you decide to send it.
  • Read your SMS history and your contact names, so it can draw your conversation list and show a thread. This reading happens on your phone.

Standard carrier message and data rates apply to every message you send or receive. That's between you and your carrier; we don't see or control it.

Incoming MMS will not arrive

While HoldOff is your default messaging app, incoming MMS is not retrieved. Picture messages and group messages will not reach you.

They won't appear in HoldOff, and they won't be sitting in your old messaging app either — Android hands incoming messages to whichever app is the default, and MMS retrieval in HoldOff is not finished.

This is not a design decision. It is an unfinished part of the app, and we would rather you heard it here, in writing, before you switch, than discover it when you miss something that mattered.

If picture messages or group threads matter to you, do not make HoldOff your default SMS app.

You can undo it

You can change your default messaging app back at any time in Android Settings (typically Settings → Apps → Default apps → SMS app, though the exact path varies by phone). Once you switch back, incoming messages go to that app again. Messages that arrived — or failed to arrive — while HoldOff was default are not retroactively recovered.

What HoldOff does not do with any of it

Your SMS history, your contact names, and your trusted-contacts list stay on your phone and are not uploaded. What does leave your phone is described in section 11 and, in full detail, in the Privacy Policy.

8. The pause can always be overridden

HoldOff never permanently blocks a message. Whatever the app says, you can always choose to send.

This is a deliberate product decision, and it belongs in the terms rather than in marketing copy, because it defines what you are and aren't buying into.

HoldOff introduces a pause. It gives you a moment and a second opinion. It does not have a veto. There is no mode, no setting and no premium tier that takes the send button away from you, locks you out of a conversation, or makes a message unsendable. If you want to send it, you send it.

Two things follow from that, and you should hold both:

  • You stay in control — of your phone, your words and your relationships. An app that could genuinely stop you from speaking would be a worse thing to install than one that can't.
  • You stay responsible. Every message that goes out is one you chose to send. HoldOff is not a safeguard against sending something harmful, and it is not a filter that failed if something you regret goes out. The decision was always yours.

11. Third parties who process your text

When you ask HoldOff to check a draft, this is what leaves your phone:

  • the draft message you're asking about;
  • up to the last 12 messages of that conversation, truncated to 4,000 characters — this includes messages the other person sent you;
  • your self-reported attachment style from the in-app quiz, if you took it;
  • for the companion chat: what you type, plus up to the last 12 turns of that chat.

It goes to our own endpoint at api.smsholdoff.com, hosted on IONOS, which forwards it to Google's Gemini API and returns the answer.

Not sent, ever: your name, phone number, email address, contact names, device identifiers, advertising ID, location, or the conversation's thread ID.

The third parties involved are Google (Gemini API), Supabase Inc. (account email, password hash and session records, in the United States) and IONOS (hosting for the site and the API).
